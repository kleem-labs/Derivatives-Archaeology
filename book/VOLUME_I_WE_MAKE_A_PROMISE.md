# Volume I — We Make a Promise

The book begins in a field because derivatives began before mathematical finance. A farmer and a baker do not need stochastic calculus to recognize that one future price can harm them in opposite directions. They need language precise enough to move that risk between them.

In this volume Mara grows wheat, Arun buys it, and the reader serves as the first contract clerk. Every shortcut will be tempting: price the promise from a forecast, treat equal dollar labels as equal value, call every price difference arbitrage, and treat forwards and futures as synonyms. Each fails for a visible reason.

## Coda — The ledger at sunset

At the end of the first volume, Mara's uncertainty has not vanished. What changed is its architecture. The forward writes opposite terminal exposures. Discounting aligns cash across dates. No-arbitrage compares complete cash-flow routes rather than opinions. Carry explains the cost of owning now instead of receiving later. Futures settlement reveals that even identical-looking final exposure can create different survival paths.

Close the pages and reconstruct one table: date 0, each intermediate margin date, and maturity. Put every cash flow into it. If any value lacks a date, currency, sign, or owner, the artifact is not ready to price.

The next volume changes one word in the contract. Arun will not be obligated to buy. He will be allowed to choose. That right creates a kink no single static stock-and-cash line can match across a continuous world.
