# Volume V — We Carry the Physical World

So far, a share could sit quietly in a drawer. Real things are heavier. Grain needs a silo. Oil needs a tank. Money has different prices at different dates. This volume puts the warehouse back into the math.

## 030 — Carry has a cost

To promise gold in six months, a dealer can buy gold now, borrow the money, insure it, store it, and hand it over later.

The future delivery price must cover that whole journey. Interest and storage push it up. Cash paid by owning the asset pulls it down. The formula is only a ledger with a short name.

[Open the workshop](../excavations/030-cost-of-carry/README.md)

## 031 — A dividend belongs to the owner

If you own a share today, you may receive its dividend before delivery day. A person who only receives the share later does not.

So a forward on the share must be cheaper than simply carrying the full share price forward. First remove the value of the dividend. Then grow what is left.

[Open the workshop](../excavations/031-dividends-and-income/README.md)

## 032 — Some things are useful just because they are nearby

An oil refinery cannot feed a futures contract into its machines. It needs real oil in a real tank.

That usefulness is called convenience yield. It is the value of having the thing when a shortage arrives. It may not show up as cash, but it belongs in the story because it changes what ownership is worth.

[Open the workshop](../excavations/032-commodities-and-convenience-yield/README.md)

## 033 — A hedge leaves one seam

A farmer sells local wheat. A futures contract may refer to wheat of a certain grade, at a certain place, on a certain date. They are close, not identical.

The small gap between cash price and futures price is called basis. A futures hedge can remove much of the big price move while leaving that smaller seam. At delivery the two prices should come close, but before then the seam can widen or narrow.

[Open the workshop](../excavations/033-basis-and-convergence/README.md)

## 034 — A small deposit can carry a large promise

A futures contract may control $100,000 of goods while asking for only $8,000 of margin. That deposit is not the price of the goods. It is a safety cushion.

If the market moves against you, money leaves your account now. A hedge can be wise at the end and still fail because the trader cannot survive the middle.

[Open the workshop](../excavations/034-margin-and-leverage/README.md)

## 035 — Match the hedge to the thing you own

A coffee producer may expect 500 pounds. A futures contract may cover 100 pounds. That tells us the rough number of contracts.

But quantity is only the first match. The right hedge also depends on how closely the producer's coffee price moves with the contract, when the crop is sold, and whether the crop actually arrives.

[Open the workshop](../excavations/035-hedging-with-futures/README.md)

## 036 — Let the data choose the amount

If cash coffee and futures do not move exactly together, one-for-one hedging is a guess.

Look at their past changes. Choose the number of futures contracts that leaves the smallest leftover wobble. This is the minimum-variance hedge ratio. It is useful, but humble: it describes the past sample, not a permanent law.

[Open the workshop](../excavations/036-minimum-variance-hedge-ratio/README.md)

## 037 — One interest rate is not enough

A payment due next week and a payment due in ten years should not be carried through time using one blunt rate.

Markets show us prices for money at many dates. Start with the nearest date, solve its discount value, then use it to solve the next date. This is bootstrapping a curve: building a ladder one rung at a time.

[Open the workshop](../excavations/037-bootstrapping-discount-curves/README.md)

## 038 — A rate for the road between two dates

If money can travel from today to June and from today to December, the two prices tell us what the June-to-December part of the journey costs.

That is a forward rate. A swap uses many such future pieces to exchange a fixed stream of payments for a floating stream. It is just two carefully dated rivers of cash made equal today.

[Open the workshop](../excavations/038-forward-rates-and-swaps/README.md)

## 039 — A right on a futures contract

Sometimes the option does not give you the physical thing. It gives you a futures position.

The same old option idea still works: a right has value because it can be used or refused. But now the object being watched is a futures price, and the contract rules—expiry, settlement, margin, and multiplier—matter as much as the formula.

[Open the workshop](../excavations/039-options-on-futures/README.md)

## What to carry with you

Whenever a forward formula looks mysterious, draw the physical journey. Who owns the thing? Who receives its income? Who pays to store it? Who needs cash before the end? The next volume tests what happens when every clean answer meets real contracts and real markets.
