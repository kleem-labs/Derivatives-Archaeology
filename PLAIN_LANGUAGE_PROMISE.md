# The Plain-Language Promise

This book is written for a reader who has never seen a derivatives formula.

Before a new word appears, the reader must have seen the thing it names. Before a formula appears, the reader must have done the calculation in ordinary language. Before a model appears, the reader must have felt the problem the model repairs.

We will say “a promise about a future price” before “derivative,” “a copy made from stock and cash” before “replication,” and “the weights that make a fair copy” before “risk-neutral probabilities.” A technical word is a label, never an explanation.

Each chapter answers one question only. It starts with a person, an object, or a choice. It ends when that choice creates the next question. If a sentence cannot be pictured, it must earn its place by making a later picture clearer.

The excavation directories are the workshop behind the book. They keep formal derivations, code, assumptions, and failure cases for readers who want to test the idea after first understanding it.

This book also makes a safety promise. It will not confuse a small premium with a small risk, a model estimate with a market forecast, or a clear explanation with a command to trade. A reader may become much better at seeing the shape of a contract in a month; nobody can honestly promise that reading alone will make the stock market predictable or profitable. The first goal is clear thinking: name the promise, map the cash flows, expose the assumptions, and stop when a fact is missing.
