# Conclusion — A Finished Foundation, an Open Market

This book stops at fifty excavations on purpose. The foundational mathematics of exchange-traded and vanilla derivatives is a coherent, bounded structure. Later research may improve models or computation, but it does not repeal discounting, state-contingent payoffs, replication, no-arbitrage, conditional expectation, or the distinction between price and forecast.

The market remains open-ended because its institutions change. New contracts create new state variables. Regulation changes collateral and funding. Electronic markets change execution. Crises reveal missing dynamics. Those developments belong in case studies or an explicitly labeled frontier volume, not in an endlessly expanding foundation.

A reader has mastered the foundation when they can take an unfamiliar contract and answer:

1. What are its cash flows in every relevant state and date?
2. Which traded portfolio reproduces or bounds them?
3. Which assumptions make that argument legal?
4. If replication fails, what extra pricing choice enters?
5. Which sensitivities and scenarios explain its risk?
6. What numerical and market evidence could falsify the implementation?

The excavation ends where responsible practice begins.
