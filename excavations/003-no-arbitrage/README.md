# 003 — No Free Lunch

Two portfolios both pay exactly $105 in one year. One costs $100; the other $101. Buy the cheaper, sell the dearer, invest the $1 difference, and the future obligations cancel. The remaining wealth required no future price forecast.

This is the law of one price: under common funding, trading, and credit assumptions, identical future cash flows require identical current prices. Its engine is **replication**—match every relevant state and date, then compare cost.

A loose slogan says arbitrage means “free money.” The useful definition is sharper: a self-financing strategy whose payoff cannot be negative and is strictly positive in at least one allowed state. Real execution can fail through spreads, financing limits, short-sale constraints, margin calls, or default.

No-arbitrage usually gives a relative price, not a prediction of where the market will go. We now apply it to delivery in the future.
