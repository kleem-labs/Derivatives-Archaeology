# 000 — A Promise About the Future

A wheat farmer fears a low harvest price; a baker fears a high one. They can agree today that one unit of wheat will change hands in six months for $6. Neither has created wheat or cash today. They have rearranged who bears the future price risk.

Calling this a “bet” misses two facts: the contract specifies an underlying observation and a rule converting that observation into cash flows. If the market price at delivery is `S_T`, the baker's long-forward value at delivery is `S_T - 6`; the farmer receives its negative. Their cash flows sum to zero before default and costs.

The first naive price is the expected future wheat price. But whose expectation, under which probabilities, and adjusted how for risk? Disagreement makes that answer unstable. The next excavations seek statements that remain true without agreeing on a forecast.

**Artifact recovered:** a derivative is a rule for future, state-dependent cash flows. Its payoff is not yet its price.
